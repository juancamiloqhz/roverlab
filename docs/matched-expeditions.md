# Matched baseline expeditions

Open a saved expedition in layout A and choose **Run matched baseline**. Stop or reset any active expedition first. The new baseline runs in the main world with the ordinary pause, speed, inspection, teaching, stop, and mission controls. It saves automatically under a new identity. After it ends, **Compare with source expedition** opens both actual results. Saved expeditions can also be selected for comparison, including after reload or import and when their conditions differ.

Reset exits the matched session and returns to normal live setup, including controller selection for a new Jev expedition. Completed records remain saved. To repeat the matched conditions, launch again from the saved source.

`createMatchedBaseline(record)` validates the source contract and verifies its history with the supported simulator before creating a fresh session. It uses the recorded world, effective scientific objective, initial mission, physical resources, duration, decision cadence, and captured intervention schedule. The baseline chooses actions at its own boundaries from its own observations. It does not execute Jev's recorded choices or its same-state alternatives. It installs no Jev controller, performs no accounting lookups, and makes no provider requests. Source records remain unchanged.

Scheduled requests retain their expedition times, locations, payloads, and ordering. Manual source requests become scheduled requests in the fresh run. Different routes can cause mission requests to take effect at different safe boundaries, and a run that ends early does not receive later requests. Comparison separates these application histories from matching external requests. Mission changes made during the fresh run remain manual events and can make its conditions differ from its source.

## Conditions and provenance

Version 12 adds optional `matchedFrom` metadata containing the immediate source record identity and version and whether its schedule was recorded or reconstructed from legacy events. New decisions retain their actual baseline version and the new record retains its simulation and cadence. Inference allowances are operational controls, shown separately from physical simulation settings. The fresh baseline consumes zero inference usage.

Versions 1 through 11 remain inspectable and replayable. A fresh baseline preserves supported legacy physical settings, including the original five-minute duration, 100-unit battery, and observation-driven decision cadence where recorded. Its baseline strategy is the current version, explicitly recorded in its decisions. Older free-text records receive no invented preset benchmark equivalence. Records before version 11 lack planned schedules, so matching reconstructs only their recorded edits and storms and labels the schedule incomplete. This limitation survives subsequent matching of a generated record. Unsupported settings or irreproducible histories produce an error while the source remains available for inspection and export.

Comparison checks scenario contents, physical settings and cadence, objective, rubric, initial preferences and requested changes, and intervention schedule. It shows effective mission histories, manual requests, controller mixtures, and versions separately. Free-text experiments disclose that the baseline uses Balanced defaults and cannot interpret arbitrary instructions. Matching external requests alone does not establish matching mission application times or a completed experiment.

## Outcomes and adherence

Delivered science and completion appear first. A timeout at base with no remaining cargo is distinct from a timeout away from base, stranding, or a manual stop. Failed or cancelled requests and usage pauses stay visible. Delivered and onboard sample counts, discoveries, inspections, energy, remaining battery, and simulated time remain separate measures. Same-state alternatives do not contribute realized results. A single pair supports observations about those runs, not a claim of consistent controller superiority.

Preset adherence uses recorded definitions and reserve settings. Whole-expedition measures report energy used, delivered science, delivery fraction, and distinct terrain cells first discovered after the initial observation per simulated minute. Zero time makes knowledge rate unavailable; no collection makes delivery fraction unavailable. Return margins use each preset decision's battery minus its cheapest offered return-route energy, compared with that decision's recorded reserve. Decisions without a return candidate are unavailable. Definitions and their limitations remain inspectable. Totals include any free-text intervals and early termination; they are not combined into an AI score.

Provider attempts, retries, unconfirmed submissions, tokens, cumulative inference wait, and estimated cost remain separate from simulated outcomes. Unknown counts retain their lower-bound labels. Missing historical Jev metadata remains unavailable. Incomplete cost shows the recorded known subtotal without repricing. Per-decision and submission details retain prompt versions, actual resolved models, prices, their capture and verification dates, and within-run changes. Monetary amounts remain estimates, not verified charges.
